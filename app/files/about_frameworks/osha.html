About OSHA (Kenya)

The Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSHA), first enacted in 2007 and updated in the Occupational Safety and Health Bill, 2024, is Kenya’s primary workplace safety law. OSHA aims to secure the safety, health, and welfare of workers and to protect persons against risks to safety and health arising from or connected with the activities of persons at work.

OSHA establishes a framework that requires employers to provide safe working conditions, monitor occupational hazards, and ensure compliance with national safety standards. It mandates technical, physical, and administrative controls across industries. Key control areas under OSHA include:

  • Safe handling and storage of hazardous substances, including corrosive liquids
  • Monitoring and control of exposure to hazardous substances, noise, and vibration
  • Mandatory risk assessment for major hazard installations
  • Permit-to-work systems for hazardous processes (e.g., confined spaces, hot work)
  • Provision and use of protective clothing, eye protection, and safety appliances
  • Medical surveillance of workers exposed to occupational risks
  • Welfare measures including safe drinking water, washing facilities, and first-aid
  • Registration and inspection of machinery such as steam boilers, hoists, and lifts
  • Emergency response planning and reporting of imminent dangers
  • Compliance with improvement and prohibition notices issued by safety officers

OSHA further empowers occupational safety and health officers with enforcement authority, including workplace inspections, sample collection, issuing notices, and ordering work stoppages in cases of imminent danger. Employers are also required to cooperate with inspections, maintain records, and ensure that employees are informed and trained on workplace hazards.

By complying with OSHA requirements, organizations can reduce workplace accidents, improve employee welfare, and meet legal obligations under Kenyan law.
